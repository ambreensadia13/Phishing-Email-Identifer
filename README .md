## Project Overview

This project documents the analysis of five example phishing emails. It demonstrates how to identify common phishing indicators, assess suspicious messages, and follow safe reporting and response practices.

The examples are provided for cybersecurity awareness and educational analysis. They should not be used to deceive recipients, collect credentials, or conduct unauthorized testing.

## Objectives

- Recognize common signs of phishing and social engineering.
- Examine suspicious sender details, links, attachments, language, and requests.
- Explain the potential risks associated with each example.
- Recommend practical actions for users who receive suspicious emails.
- Provide a repeatable checklist for reviewing future messages.

## Analysis Method

Each example is reviewed using the following indicators:

- **Sender identity:** Does the sender address or domain match the organization it claims to represent?
- **Urgency or pressure:** Does the message threaten consequences or demand immediate action?
- **Links:** Does the displayed link differ from the actual destination or point to an unfamiliar domain?
- **Attachments:** Is an unexpected file included, especially one asking the recipient to enable macros or run content?
- **Requests for sensitive information:** Does the email ask for passwords, verification codes, payment, or personal data?
- **Language and context:** Are there unusual errors, generic greetings, unexpected requests, or inconsistencies?
- **Verification:** Can the request be confirmed through an independent, trusted channel?

No single indicator proves that an email is malicious. Consider the full context and verify unusual requests independently.

## Safety Recommendations

- Do not click suspicious links or open unexpected attachments.
- Never share passwords or one-time verification codes by email.
- Visit an organization's official website by typing its address yourself, rather than using a link in a suspicious message.
- Confirm payment, account, or urgent requests through a known, trusted contact method.
- Report suspected phishing using your organization's approved process or email provider's reporting feature.
- If you interacted with a suspicious message, contact your IT/security team promptly and follow its incident-response guidance.
